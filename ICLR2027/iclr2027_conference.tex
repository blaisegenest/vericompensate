\section{Conclusion}
In this paper, we tackled real-time (few ms) certification for DNN robustness, which has never been performed before to the best of our knowledge. The main idea we develop is to proceed in 2 steps: first, compute {\em once, offline} global robustness bounds $\bar{\beta}$ on the DNN. Then use a very simplistic look-up table based on $\bar{\beta}$ to perform the {\em online} certification.	We developed 2 original techniques of independent interest, necessary to obtain offline bounds $\bar{\beta}$ good enough to certify $>70\%$ of images. 

First, the LAE-DNN architecture, which filters out non-ID features, avoiding very large $\beta$ associated with 
non-ID inputs (see Fig. \ref{fig3}). On vanilla DNN architecture, real-time certification would not be possible (lower bounds imply that), irrespective of the computational method used.
Second, we developed the new {\em Diff} MILP method, which is more accurate for global robustness than the classical MILP encoding. This results in $25\%$ lower bounds in average than the state-of-the-art ITNE model \cite{ITNE}. Following \cite{diff}, the idea is to look at (small) {\em diff}erential variables $y$ natively rather than represent them as the difference of 2 larger variables $x,x'$.

This is a concept and feasibility paper, showing for the first time, to our knowledge, the feasibility 
of real-time certification. Many implementation details are out of the scope of the paper: testing many LAE algorithms, possibly non-linear, non-MILP techniques, more activation functions, larger DNNs, etc.
These are left for future work, as there is no theoretical limitations of our two steps technique.
